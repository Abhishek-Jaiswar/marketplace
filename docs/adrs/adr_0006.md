# ADR 0006: Marketplace Commerce Domain, From Checkout to Seller Payout

- **Status**: Proposed
- **Date**: 2026-11-10

---

## Context

The existing schema covers identity, seller onboarding, catalog and inventory. It stops where a marketplace starts earning: nothing can be bought, shipped, returned, invoiced or paid out. The storefront runs on mock data for the same reason, and every screen planned for the redesign depends on this domain existing.

CBS is a multi-seller, general-merchandise marketplace for India. That fixes several requirements: rupee pricing against a printed MRP, GST invoices issued by each seller, prepaid and cash-on-delivery orders, and tax collected by the platform as an e-commerce operator.

## Decision

### 1. One checkout, one order per seller

`Order` is what the shopper bought in one checkout. It splits into one `SellerOrder` per seller, which owns acceptance, dispatch deadline, invoice and settlement. `OrderItem` holds the lines.

Sellers act independently, so a single order status cannot describe a cart with two sellers. Shoppers see `Order`; sellers see only their `SellerOrder`.

```
Order ── SellerOrder ── OrderItem ── ShipmentItem ── Shipment
              │             └── ReturnRequest ── Refund
              └── Invoice, SellerLedgerEntry ── Payout
```

### 2. Orders record facts, they do not reference them

`OrderItem` copies title, SKU, image, price, tax and commission at the moment of sale. Addresses are stored on `Order` as JSON. A later catalog edit or commission change cannot rewrite history. Foreign keys from commerce records use `Restrict`, so a listing or seller with order history cannot be hard-deleted.

### 3. Sellers are paid from an append-only ledger

Every amount owed to or by a seller is a `SellerLedgerEntry`: sale, commission, fees, TCS, TDS, reversal, payout. `idempotencyKey` prevents double posting. `availableAt` holds funds until the return window closes, and a `Payout` draws the balance down. This mirrors the inventory ledger from ADR 0005.

### 4. Money is integer paise

Rupee amounts lose precision in tax calculation, so all new money columns hold integer paise and rates hold basis points (1800 = 18%). Order-level amounts are `Int`; seller balances and payouts are `BigInt` because they accumulate without an upper bound.

`SellerListing.mrpPrice` and `sellingPrice` have no defined unit today. They become paise when the catalog module is migrated, in one change with its API and screens.

### 5. External events are idempotent

Payment webhooks are stored with a unique `(provider, providerEventId)`, and checkout carries an `idempotencyKey`. Retries and redelivery cannot create a second order or capture.

### 6. Reviews are verified by construction

`ProductReview` is one-to-one with an `OrderItem`, so only a buyer can review, once per purchase. Rating totals are denormalised on `Product` and `Seller` for listing pages.

### 7. Catalog becomes filterable and legally complete

`CategoryAttribute` defines what sellers must enter and what shoppers can filter on. `Product` gains HSN code, GST rate, country of origin and manufacturer details. A full-text index backs search. `Collection` gives admins curated storefront shelves.

### 8. The database enforces the invariants that matter

Check constraints cover stock (`reserved <= onHand`), price (`sellingPrice <= mrpPrice`), ratings and positive quantities. Application bugs fail loudly instead of corrupting stock or money.

## Lifecycles

| Entity | Path |
|---|---|
| `Order` | `PENDING_PAYMENT` to `PLACED` to `COMPLETED`; or `PAYMENT_FAILED`, `CANCELLED` |
| `SellerOrder` | `NEW`, `ACCEPTED`, `PACKED`, `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED`; or `CANCELLED` |
| `ReturnRequest` | `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `PICKUP_SCHEDULED`, `PICKED_UP`, `RECEIVED`, `COMPLETED`; or `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` |
| `Payout` | `PENDING`, `PROCESSING`, `PAID`; or `FAILED`, `ON_HOLD` |

Every order transition writes an `OrderEvent`. Every privileged action writes an `AuditLog`.

## What each surface gets

| Surface | Screens |
|---|---|
| Storefront | Home, category with filters, search, product, store, cart, checkout, confirmation, orders, order tracking, returns, wishlist, addresses, account |
| Seller Central | Today, orders, returns, products and listings, inventory, payouts and ledger, invoices, coupons, ratings, settings, onboarding |
| Admin Central | Seller and catalog queues, orders, returns, payouts, commission rules, categories and attributes, brands, coupons, collections, review moderation, customers, audit log |

## Build order

Each module follows the inventory pattern and ships with its screens.

1. Catalog migration, including paise, filters and search.
2. Cart, checkout, orders, with Razorpay and cash on delivery.
3. Seller order processing and shipping.
4. Returns and refunds.
5. Invoices, ledger and payouts.
6. Reviews, coupons, collections.

## Deliberately left out

Phone OTP sign-in, a carrier integration, platform-run fulfilment, shopper-seller messaging, product Q&A, support tickets, loyalty and sponsored listings. Each can be added without reshaping the tables above. `Shipment.carrier` is free text until a carrier is chosen.

## Open items

- **Tax review:** TCS, TDS, invoice numbering and place-of-supply rules need sign-off from a chartered accountant before real money moves. Rates must live in configuration.
- **Bank details:** `BankAccount.accountNumber` is stored in plain text and needs encryption before launch.
- **Reservation expiry:** nothing releases expired reservations, and commit does not check `expiresAt`. Checkout needs both.

## Consequences

- Additive migration: every change to an existing table is nullable or defaulted.
- 29 new tables to build services for, sequenced above. Tables stay unused until their module ships.
- Each seller's order flow can change independently of the shopper's view, at the cost of an extra level in every order query.
